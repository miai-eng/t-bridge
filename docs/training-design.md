# Intercultural training: design notes

## What the questionnaire is

A short, self-reported needs survey. It asks about:

- prior childcare training and experience
- languages used and comfort level
- concrete situations the person finds difficult (e.g. handovers, parent communication, daily routines, safety practices)

It is **not** a psychological or personality assessment and does not produce a score or type. It does not reproduce or imitate any commercial intercultural assessment.

## What the generated training looks like

- Built from workplace scenarios (e.g. "a parent hands over a note in Japanese at drop-off")
- Explains the *reason* behind a practice, so staff can apply it in new situations
- Suggests concrete next actions

## Guardrails against stereotyping

LLM-generated cross-cultural content tends to drift into generalizations such as "Japanese people do X; Westerners do Y." Generated content should:

- describe practices and contexts, not national character
- be reviewed against a checklist before being shown to staff
- allow staff to flag content as inaccurate or stereotyped

Defining and enforcing this checklist is one of the main design challenges of the project.
